Locator

Find where they are being held

You can look this up yourself using the official ICE locator. It is free and it never asks for payment. Here is how, step by step.

→ Open the official ICE locatorlocator.ice.gov · free
  1. 1

    Find their A-Number, if you can.

    This is a nine-digit number that looks like A## ### ### on any immigration document they have — a notice, an ID card, or court papers. You don’t strictly need it, but it is the most reliable way to search.
  2. 2

    Open the official locator only.

    Use the ICE Online Detainee Locator at locator.ice.gov. It is free. No legitimate search site asks you to pay to find someone — if one does, close it.
  3. 3

    Search in one of two ways.

    By A-Number and country of birth — or by first name, last name, country of birth, and date of birth. Spell the name exactly as it appears on their documents, and try a middle name or a second surname if the first search finds nothing.
  4. 4

    Write down the facility.

    If they are found, note the exact facility name, city, and state. You will need this for phone calls, sending money, mail, and visits.
  5. 5

    Find the facility’s phone and address.

    Search the facility’s name for its phone number and mailing address. Keep both somewhere the whole family can see them — the next steps (calls and money) use them.
  6. 6

    If they are not found yet.

    Someone just detained may not appear for 24 to 48 hours. People under 18, or those held by a different agency or in a local jail, may not be listed at all.

    Wait a day and search again, and contact the nearest ICE field office to ask. Do not pay anyone who promises to “find” them faster.

A note on safety

This walkthrough uses free, public government tools. Detention Navigator is not part of the government and never charges to search for someone. If anyone asks you to pay to locate a person, treat it as a warning sign.
